You finish with a written strategy and a 90-day roadmap your team can run, not a logo or a posting calendar. The work covers positioning, audience, platforms, content pillars and the order to do things in.

Questions

Is this a rebrand or a marketing plan?

Neither, though it can lead to both. It’s the decision work that comes first: who you serve, what you stand for, which platforms deserve your time and what to stop doing. You finish with a written strategy and a 90-day roadmap.

What do you need from me before we start?

Access to your analytics and social accounts, your last few campaigns (the good and the bad), your goals for the next year, and the name of whoever makes the final call. Strategy stalls when the decision-maker isn’t in the room.

Can you help a business that sells offline, like a venue or a shop?

Yes. Mana Bay, Bangladesh’s first premium water park, is the example. The problem was explaining a premium ticket price to a market used to low-cost parks. The work ran across Meta, influencer reels and an anniversary giveaway, and average daily website visitors grew from under 12 to roughly 2,200 in six months.

How do you choose which platforms to focus on?

By where your audience already pays attention and which platforms your team can keep up. In Bangladesh that often means Facebook and YouTube first, but I check against your own data before recommending anything.

What happens after the roadmap?

You can run it with your own team, bring my team in to carry out parts of it, or move to a Strategic Partner arrangement so I stay close to the decisions. None is required.

How will we know it’s working?

We agree two or three numbers before we begin, such as qualified enquiries, audience growth on the chosen platforms or bookings, so you can check the roadmap against them at the 90-day mark.
